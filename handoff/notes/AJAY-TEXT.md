# Ajay's comeback: story text (draft for the STORY block, chapter "ajay")

Tone: a reckless founder, kept light. Short lines (one sentence each, under about 90 characters). Speakers: `partner` (Ajay's business partner, Priya, the one who reads the spreadsheets), `customer` (whoever pays for the current contract), `notebook` (Austin's notebook, 1958). The player is Ajay. No game numbers in the text: amounts, months and dates come from the back end and are shown beside the text, never inside it.

## Intro (when a comeback starts), six beats

1. partner: "Ajay Albert. Last year you borrowed a fortune to start Albert Orbital."
2. girl (Mara): "I'm leaving, Ajay. My things are in the car. I can't do another launch party." (2.1: parents disown him in beat 3)
3. partner: "The lenders want it back by the deadline, or they take your grandfather's workshop."
4. notebook: "To whoever opens this: build it cheap, build it right, and bring the data home."
5. customer: "Iron Eclipse charges a fortune for data. Bring it cheaper and we will pay."
6. partner: "Pick a contract. Every satellite that delivers pays down the debt."

## Why Ajay keeps going (one shown on the hub, rotating by flight count)

- "The lenders don't want satellites. They want their money."
- "Mum and Dad's house is on the loan. They won't pick up."
- "Iron Eclipse bought your patents for pocket change. They're laughing."
- "Grandpa Austin was right about all of this. Somebody has to prove it."
- "Albert Orbital was a joke. Albert Orbital 2 doesn't have to be."
- "Customers are tired of Iron Eclipse prices. That's your opening."

## Screen lines (the hub and the seven screens)

- hub (no flights yet): partner: "Three contracts on the table. Pick one you can actually deliver."
- hub (after a paid flight): partner: "That one paid. Pay some debt, or fly again while the interest ticks."
- hub (debt under half of the start): partner: "Halfway there. The lenders have stopped calling twice a day."
- hub (deadline close, a quarter of the months or less left): partner: "Clock's nearly out. Fly something cheap and sure."
- briefing: customer: (the contract's own reason, from `data/contracts.js`)
- orbit: partner: "The customer pays for data, not for a pretty orbit."
- build: notebook: "Every part costs money and weight. Fit only what the job needs."
- launcher: partner: "The cheapest rocket that can carry it. We are not made of money. Literally."
- review: partner: "A failed launch costs the whole satellite. Check the list."
- mission: partner: "Fingers crossed. The data pays only if it reaches the ground."
- debrief, contract paid: customer: "Data received. Payment sent. Pleasure doing business."
- debrief, paid in part: customer: "Not everything we asked for. We paid for what arrived."
- debrief, nothing paid: customer: "Nothing new in that data. We can't pay for it."

## Endings

- won: partner: "Debt paid. The workshop is yours." notebook: "Well done. Now build the next one."
- lost by time: partner: "The deadline passed. The lenders took the keys." notebook: "Failure is a lesson. Try again."
- lost by cash: partner: "Nothing left to launch with. The lenders took the keys." notebook: "Build cheaper. Try again."
